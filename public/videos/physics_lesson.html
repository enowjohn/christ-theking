What is Motion?

Motion is the change in position of an object with respect to time.

Motion = Change in Position / Time

Types of motion:

  • Linear motion (straight line)
  • Circular motion (in circles)
  • Oscillatory motion (back and forth)
  • Rotational motion (spinning)

Speed and Velocity

Speed

Speed = Distance / Time

Scalar quantity (only magnitude)

Example: 60 km/h

Velocity

Velocity = Displacement / Time

Vector quantity (magnitude and direction)

Example: 60 km/h North

Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

a = (v₂ - v₁) / t

Example:

A car accelerates from 0 to 60 km/h in 10 seconds

a = (60 - 0) / 10 = 6 km/h/s

Force

Force is a push or pull that can change an object's motion.

Force = Mass × Acceleration
F = ma

Unit: Newton (N)

1 N = 1 kg × 1 m/s²

Newton's Laws of Motion

First Law (Inertia)

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an external force.

Second Law (F = ma)

The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.

Third Law (Action-Reaction)

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Gravity

Gravity is the force that attracts objects toward the center of the Earth.

Weight = Mass × g
W = mg

Where g = 9.8 m/s² (acceleration due to gravity)

Example:

A person with mass 70 kg has weight:

W = 70 × 9.8 = 686 N

Friction

Friction is a force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact.

Types of Friction:

  • Static friction (prevents motion)
  • Kinetic friction (opposes motion)
  • Rolling friction (wheels)
  • Air resistance (moving through air)

Friction can be both helpful (brakes) and harmful (wastes energy)

Work and Energy

Work

Work = Force × Distance

W = F × d

Unit: Joule (J)

Kinetic Energy

KE = ½mv²

Energy of motion

Potential Energy

PE = mgh

Energy due to position

Simple Machines

Simple machines make work easier by multiplying force or changing direction.

Types:

  • Lever (seesaw, scissors)
  • Pulley (elevators, cranes)
  • Inclined plane (ramps)
  • Wheel and axle (cars)
  • Screw (drills)

Practice Problems

Try these:

1. A car travels 120 km in 2 hours. What is its average speed?

2. What force is needed to accelerate a 1000 kg car at 3 m/s²?

3. Calculate the weight of a 60 kg person.

4. How much work is done to lift a 10 kg box 2 meters?
